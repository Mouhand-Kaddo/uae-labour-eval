## OQ-012: Which notice period applies when the contract sets one outside 30–90 days, and what does pay in lieu use?

### Takeaway
No official source, MOHRE guidance or court judgment found addresses an out-of-range contractual notice period. The Law points to a split answer:
- **A term that hurts the worker in the termination at hand** (too short when the employer terminates, too long when the worker resigns) is void under Art 65(3). The statutory limit (30 or 90 days) then applies.
- **A term that helps the worker in that termination** (longer when the employer terminates, shorter when the worker resigns) is arguably saved by the "more beneficial" exception in Art 65(3) and by Art 43(2), which lets the notice period differ between the parties "in the interest of the Worker."

Pay in lieu follows whichever period applies. Confidence: low to medium. This is a reading of the text, not settled practice.

### Cited findings
- **Art 43(1) (EN) [PRIMARY]:** either party may terminate "provided that the other party is notified in writing, and commits to work within the warning period agreed upon in the contract, provided that the period is not less than thirty (30) days, and not more than ninety (90) days." — [Law EN](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541/download)
- **Art 43(1) (AR) [PRIMARY]:** "والالتزام بالعمل خلال مدة الإنذار المتفق عليها في العقد، على ألا تقل المدة عن (30) ثلاثين يومًا، ولا تزيد على (90) تسعين يومًا." The rule is the contractually agreed period, bounded 30–90. — [Law AR](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1541/download)
- **Art 43(2) [PRIMARY]**
  - EN: "An agreement may be made to exempt from the warning condition or reduce its duration while preserving all the rights of the Worker for the warning period agreed upon in the employment contract. The warning period must be the same for both parties unless it is in the interest of the Worker."
  - AR: "ويشترط أن تكون مدة الإنذار واحدة للطرفين ما لم تكن لصالح العامل".
  - So the Law expressly allows an asymmetric period that favours the worker.
  - — [Law EN](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541/download); [Law AR](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1541/download)
- **Art 43(3)–(4) [PRIMARY]**
  - The party that does not observe the warning period pays a warning allowance "even if the failure to warn does not result in harm to the other party", equal to "the Worker's wage for the entire warning period or the remaining part of it". AR: "تعويضًا مساويًّا لأجر العامل عن مدة الإنذار كلها أو الجزء المتبقي منها".
  - It is calculated "according to the last wage received by the Worker" (daily average for piece-rate workers).
  - Pay in lieu is defined only by reference to "the warning period". The text does not say which period that is when the contract figure is out of range.
  - — [Law EN](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541/download)
- **Art 65(1) [PRIMARY]:** the rights in the Law "represent the minimum rights for Workers" and do not prejudice contract rights "that are more beneficial than the rights established under the provisions of this Decree by law." — [Law EN](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541/download)
- **Art 65(3) [PRIMARY]**
  - EN: "Every condition that violates the provisions of this Decree by law ... shall be considered null unless it is more beneficial to the Worker".
  - AR: "يعد باطلاً كل شرط يخالف أحكام هذا المرسوم بقانون، ولو كان سابقًا على نفاذه، ما لم يكن أكثر فائدة للعامل".
  - — [Law AR](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1541/download)
- **Art 65(6) [PRIMARY], for comparison:** contracts concluded before the Law came into force use "not less than" floors only: 30 days for under 5 years' service, 60 days for over 5 years, 90 days for over 10 years. There is no ceiling. — [Law EN](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541/download)
- **The ER do not address this [PRIMARY].** ER Art 10(1) only requires the contract to state the "notice period". No ER article in the ingested index deals with an out-of-range notice period. — [ER EN](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1547/download)
- **u.ae [official portal, secondary restatement]:** the notice period "must not be less than 30 days and not exceed 90 days"; it "must be the same for both parties unless it is in favour of the worker"; it may be "reduced or exempted on mutual agreement"; and the notice allowance "is calculated according to the last wage received by the worker." The page gives no rule for out-of-range clauses. — [u.ae: Terminating employment contracts](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/Sector-of-employment/employment-in-the-private-sector/terminating-employment-contracts)
- **Chambers & Partners, 20 Jan 2022 [SECONDARY]:**
  - Relies on Art 65: "the conditions that are more beneficial to the employee shall apply."
  - Says an employer cannot stipulate a shorter notice period and still require the employee to serve the statutory term.
  - Does not say what happens below 30 or above 90 days and cites no case law.
  - — [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/the-notice-period-under-uae-federal-decree-by-law-no-33-of-2021-regulating-labour-relations)
- **Al Ramsy Advocates, "legally reviewed" 14 Feb 2026 [SECONDARY]:** "Any clause exceeding 90 days is generally considered invalid." It does not say what replaces the clause, gives no below-30 rule for confirmed employees, cites no cases and does not mention Art 65(3). — [Al Ramsy](https://alramsyadvocates.com/notice-period-uae/)
- **Other commentary [SECONDARY]:** a search summary reported other guides saying a clause above 90 days is "void, and the maximum 90 days applies instead". The underlying page was not identified or verified, so this is not relied on. No Dubai or Abu Dhabi Court of Cassation or Federal Supreme Court judgment under Law 33/2021 on this point was found in English or Arabic searches. — searches logged; none citable

### Inferences
- **Readings and arguments**
  - **R1: nearest legal limit (15 becomes 30, 120 becomes 90) for both parties.**
    - For: simple; matches the commentary saying a clause over 90 days is "invalid"; treats 43(1) as a mandatory band protecting both sides.
    - Against: ignores Art 65(3)'s "unless it is more beneficial to the Worker" exception and Art 43(2)'s allowance for an asymmetric period in the worker's interest.
  - **R2: worker-favourable split by who terminates (recommended).** Art 65(3) voids an out-of-range term only where it is not more beneficial to the worker, and the benefit depends on who is terminating.
    - **Contract says 15 days:**
      - Employer terminates: 15 days is worse for the worker, so the term is void and 30 days applies. Pay in lieu = 30 days' wage (or the unserved part).
      - Worker resigns: 15 days is better for the worker, so it is arguably saved. The worker owes 15 days' notice and at most 15 days' wage in lieu.
    - **Contract says 120 days:**
      - Employer terminates: 120 days is better for the worker (longer paid notice), so it is arguably saved. Pay in lieu = 120 days' wage.
      - Worker resigns: 120 days is worse for the worker, so the term is void and 90 days caps the worker's obligation and liability.
    - Against: Art 43(1) reads as a mandatory band and Art 65(3) is about the Law's own provisions. A court could hold that the 30-day floor also protects the employer, but the text gives no employer-protective exception.
  - **R3: the clause is void entirely and there is no agreed period.** Least supported. Art 43(1) still requires notice, so the floor or ceiling would be read in anyway. In practice this collapses into R1 or R2.
- **Pay in lieu:** Art 43(3) ties the allowance to "the warning period", the one that legally governs that termination. Under any reading it uses the period found to apply, times the last wage (Art 43(4)), for the whole period or the unserved remainder.
- **Who terminates matters only under R2**, which is also the only reading that gives effect to the "more beneficial" words in both Art 43(2) and Art 65(3).
- **Recommendation for the tool:** implement R2 and keep returning OQ-012 with every result. Optionally also show the R1 figure, since R1 and R2 agree whenever the out-of-range term hurts the worker. They differ only in the two worker-favourable cases: employer termination with more than 90 days, and resignation with fewer than 30.
- **Confidence:**
  - Medium that an out-of-range term hurting the worker is replaced by the nearest limit.
  - Low that a worker-favourable out-of-range term is enforced. There is no authority either way.

### Gaps
- No court judgment under Law 33/2021 (Federal Supreme Court, Dubai or Abu Dhabi Cassation) on an out-of-range notice clause was found. Older case law under Law 8/1980 was not reviewed.

## OQ-013: Is a six-month probation 180 days or six calendar months?

### Takeaway
Art 67 says the Law's periods are counted "according to the Gregorian calendar" and that "the month shall be deemed as thirty (30) days" in applying the Law. Read literally, the six-month maximum in Art 9(1) is 180 days. No MOHRE, u.ae, court or commentary source was found that addresses the point. The practical difference is 1–4 days.

Recommendation: keep option A (180 days) as the main rule and flag OQ-013. Confidence: medium, because the text is clear but there is no confirming practice.

### Cited findings
- **Art 9(1) [PRIMARY]**
  - EN: the employer may appoint the worker on probation "for a period not exceeding six (6) months as of the date of commencing work".
  - AR: "مدة لا تزيد على (6) ستة أشهر من تاريخ مباشرة العمل".
  - — [Law EN](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541/download); [Law AR](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1541/download)
- **Art 67 (EN) [PRIMARY]:** "The periods and dates referred to in this Decree by law shall be calculated according to the Gregorian calendar; and in the implementation of this Decree by law, a calendar year shall be deemed as three hundred and sixty-five (365) days, and the month shall be deemed as thirty (30) days." — [Law EN](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541/download)
- **Art 67 (AR) [PRIMARY]:** "يكون حساب المدد والمواعيد المنصوص عليها في هذا المرسوم بقانون بالتقويم الميلادي، وتعد السنة الميلادية في تطبيق أحكام هذا المرسوم بقانون (365) ثلاثمائة وخمس وستون يومًا والشهر (30) ثلاثون يومًا."
  - The 30-day rule is phrased "in applying the provisions of this Decree-Law" ("في تطبيق أحكام هذا المرسوم بقانون") and covers every period "المنصوص عليها" (stipulated in) the Law, which includes Art 9's six months.
  - — [Law AR](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1541/download)
- **The ER do not address this [PRIMARY].** ER Art 10(1) lists "probation period if available" as a contract term. ER Arts 11 and 12(5)(b) deal with consequences of ending a contract during probation, not its length. — [ER EN](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1547/download)
- **Commentary [SECONDARY]:** Lexology, HR blogs and Khaleej Times state only "up to six months from the start of work" and do not convert this to days. The Arabic commentary found (e.g. hhslawyers.com) says the maximum is "6 أشهر" and may be extended by agreement within that total, but gives no day count.
  - — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=dcaef5ba-a5bd-45a4-8189-ef3b73abca9a); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/legal/uae-jobs-what-you-need-to-know-about-6-month-probation-period-after-new-employment-law-went-into-fo?amp=1); [HHS Lawyers (AR)](https://hhslawyers.com/ar/blog/%D9%81%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%B1%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%85%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%8A/)
  - No source found applies Art 67's 30-day month to probation, or rejects it.
- **No court judgment found [SECONDARY].** An Arabic search for Dubai Cassation rulings on probation exceeding six months returned only press and blog items, mostly about Dubai Government HR Law 8/2018, which is out of scope. — [Al Bayan, 2018 (Dubai Govt HR, not private sector)](https://www.albayan.ae/across-the-uae/news-and-reports/2018-07-26-1.3322700)

### Inferences
- **Reading A: 180 days.**
  - For: Art 67's deeming rule is specific ("the month shall be deemed as thirty (30) days"), is applied "in the implementation of this Decree by law", and covers all periods in the Law. A specific deeming rule ordinarily controls the general "Gregorian calendar" instruction. The Gregorian clause reads as excluding the Hijri calendar, not as requiring calendar-month counting.
  - Consistency: the project already uses 30-day months for gratuity and leave (OQ-005, OQ-011 at 180 days). Using 180 here keeps the tools consistent.
- **Reading B: six calendar months.**
  - For: "calculated according to the Gregorian calendar" may be read as requiring calendar-month counting for periods expressed in months. The 30-day deeming would then serve only arithmetic, such as pro-rating wages, leave and gratuity.
  - Against: the Law does not say this.
- **Size of the gap:** six calendar months from day 1 run 181–184 days (computed):
  - 1 Jan–30 Jun: 181 days, or 182 in a leap year
  - 1 Mar–31 Aug: 184 days
  - 1 Sep–28 Feb: 181 days
- **Risk direction:**
  - Treating 180 days as the maximum never lets an employer exceed either reading, so A is the conservative answer for "is this probation lawful?".
  - For "was termination on day 182 still within probation?", A says no and B may say yes. Only edge cases are affected.
- **Recommendation:** keep A. Keep `depends_on: [OQ-013]` only on cases whose end date falls between day 181 and six calendar months. Cases fully inside 180 days are unaffected by OQ-013, so the dependency could be narrowed to the 181–184-day window.

### Gaps
- MOHRE's practice could not be checked because mohre.gov.ae was unreachable. Unknown: whether its e-contract or work-permit system records probation in months or days, and whether it auto-confirms at 6 calendar months or 180 days.
- No court judgment on day-counting for probation under Law 33/2021 or Law 8/1980 was found.
- Law-firm commentary (Al Tamimi, BSA, Hadef, Clyde & Co, Pinsent Masons) on this point was not found.

## OQ-007: Art 60 fine, English "two million AED (1,000,000)" versus Arabic one million, after Decree-Law 9/2024

### Takeaway
The official Arabic consolidated text of Art 60(1) sets the fine at AED 100,000 to AED 1,000,000 ("مليون درهم").

The English "two million" is a translation slip. Within the English text itself it conflicts with:
- its own numeral "(1,000,000)";
- Art 60(2), which says "AED (1,000,000) one million";
- the 2024 commentary.

Use AED 1,000,000. Confidence: high.

The consolidated text the project ingested already contains the Art 60 as amended by Decree-Law 9/2024, including the new fictitious-employment clauses (2)–(4). The standalone 2024 Decree-Law text could not be retrieved.

### Cited findings
- **Art 60(1) (AR, consolidated) [PRIMARY]:** "يُعاقب بالغرامة التي لا تقل عن (100,000) مائة ألف درهم ولا تزيد على (1,000,000) مليون درهم، كل من: أ. استخدم عاملاً لم يصرح له بالعمل لديه. ب. استقدم أو استخدم عاملاً، وتركه دون أن يعمل. ج. استعمل تصاريح العمل في غير الغرض المخصص لإصدارها. د. أغلق منشأة أو أوقف نشاطها دون اتخاذ إجراءات تسوية حقوق العاملين ... ه. قام بتشغيل حدث ... و. من وافق على تشغيل الحدث ..." — [Law AR](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1541/download)
- **Art 60(1) (EN, consolidated) [PRIMARY]:** "A fine not less than on hundred thousand AED (100,000) and not exceeding two million AED (1,000,000) shall be imposed on any person who: ..." The typo "on hundred" is in the source. — [Law EN](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541/download)
- **Art 60(2) [PRIMARY]**
  - EN: "Any employer who circumvents the provisions of the laws, regulations or resolutions regulating the labor market and appoints one or more workers in a fictitious manner shall be punished by a fine of not less than AED (100,000) one hundred thousand and not more than AED (1,000,000) one million."
  - Also in 60(2): the court orders the employer to return financial incentives to the government body, the employer cannot recover them from the worker, and "The penalty ... shall be multiplied by the number of workers who were appointed in a fictitious manner."
  - AR: "... ولا تزيد على (1,000,000) مليون درهم ... وتتعدد العقوبة المنصوص عليها في هذا البند بتعدد العمال الذين تم تعيينهم بشكل صوري."
  - — [Law EN](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541/download); [Law AR](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1541/download)
- **Art 60(3)–(4) [PRIMARY]:** criminal action for the 60(2) offence only "at the request of the Minister or his delegate". MOHRE may settle before judgment for "not less than (50%) of the minimum value of the fine" plus return of all incentives; the case then lapses. — [Law EN](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541/download)
- **DLA Piper, 28 Aug 2024 (D. Carlin, V. Smylie) [SECONDARY]**
  - A Federal Decree-Law announced 12 Aug 2024, effective 31 Aug 2024, "amends various provisions of the Labour Law relating to penalties."
  - Employers "face an increase in fines ranging from AED100,000 to AED1 million" for employing people without authorisation, misusing work permits, closing a business without settling workers' rights, and illegally employing minors. These match Art 60(1)(a), (c), (d) and (e).
  - New penalties for fictitious employment, including fictitious Emiratisation, are multiplied per person.
  - It does not name Decree-Law 9/2024 or give the article number.
  - — [DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2024/UAE-government-introduces-increased-fines-and-criminal-penalties-for-violations-of-the-UAE-Labour-Law-)
- **Paoletti [SECONDARY]:** the amended Art 60 makes employers return government financial incentives for each fictitious hire and bars recovery from the employee. — [Paoletti](https://paoletti.com/?p=10786)
- **Velmont Crest [SECONDARY]:** prosecution only at the request of the Minister or a delegate. — [Velmont Crest](https://velmontcrest.ae/insights/uae-labour-law-fines-employer-guide/)
- **Related but separate [SECONDARY, unverified]:** Cabinet Decision 43 of 2025 reportedly sets administrative fines for fake Emiratisation (AED 20,000–100,000 per person). This is a separate administrative regime, not Art 60. — [UPPERSETUP](https://uppersetup.com/en/article/emiratisation-2026-the-complete-employer-guide-quotas)
- **MOHRE's consolidated PDFs [PRIMARY, not readable]:** MOHRE publishes a consolidated English PDF, "Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 Regarding the Regulation of Employment Relationship and its amendments", and an Arabic one, "مرسوم بقانون اتحادي رقم 33 لسنة 2021 بشأن تنظيم علاقات العمل وتعديلاته". Both timed out, so the MOHRE English translation of Art 60(1) could not be compared. — [MOHRE EN PDF](https://www.mohre.gov.ae/assets/download/e82f7872/Federal%20Decree-Law%20No.%2033%20of%202021%20Regarding%20the%20Regulation%20of%20Employment%20Relationship%20and%20its%20amendments_638990571068264034.pdf.aspx); [MOHRE AR PDF](https://mohre.gov.ae/assets/download/43e39236/%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%B3%D9%88%D9%85%20%D8%A8%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86%20%D8%A7%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%AF%D9%8A%20%D8%B1%D9%82%D9%85%2033%20%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9%202021%20%D8%A8%D8%B4%D8%A3%D9%86%20%D8%AA%D9%86%D8%B8%D9%8A%D9%85%20%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%AA%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%85%D9%84%20%D9%88%D8%AA%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%87_638990570577815564.pdf.aspx)

### Inferences
- **Every indicator supports AED 1,000,000:**
  - the prevailing Arabic words and numerals;
  - the English numeral;
  - the parallel English clause 60(2), "one million";
  - DLA Piper's "AED100,000 to AED1 million" for the same 60(1) offences.
- The only contrary token is the English word "two". This is a translation error, of the same kind as the "on hundred" typo in the same sentence.
- **Recommendation:** decide OQ-007 as "Art 60(1) maximum = AED 1,000,000 (Arabic prevails)". `cite` cases should quote the Arabic or the numeral and should not penalise answers that say one million.
- The consolidated text already reflects the 2024 amendment: clauses 2–4 on fictitious employment and incentive clawback appear in both languages. No later amendment to Art 60 through October 2026 was found, but this was not exhaustively checked.

  - DLA Piper implies the range was increased but gives no old figures.
- Unknown whether "two million" appears in MOHRE's own English translation or only in the uaelegislation.gov.ae English consolidation.
