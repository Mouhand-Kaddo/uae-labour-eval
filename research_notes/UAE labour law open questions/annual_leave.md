## OQ-004: Is the Arabic-only Art 29(9) rule (pro-rata leave pay for part years, on basic wage) operative, and does MOHRE reflect it?

### Takeaway
Yes. This is the best-supported finding in these notes (high confidence). The sentence is in the Arabic Law, and MOHRE's own English translation of the Law, MOHRE's "Dear Worker" guidance and the u.ae English and Arabic pages all state it. The English text that lacks it is a translation gap. Answers in English should state the rule, and English `cite` cases should expect pro-rata leave pay for part years, calculated on the basic wage.

### Cited Findings
- **[P]** The Arabic Art 29(9) contains "كما يستحق أجرة الإجازة عن أجزاء السنة بنسبة ما قضاه منها في العمل، وتحسب وفق الأجر الأساسي". The ingested English Art 29(9) omits it (`labour-law:ar:29:2` and `labour-law:en:29:2` in `data/index/chunks.jsonl`).
- **[P]** MOHRE's English PDF "Federal Decree-Law No. (33) of 2021 Regarding the Regulation of Employment Relationships and its amendments" (58 pages, downloaded 2026-10-18) renders Art 29(9) as: "The worker shall be entitled to a wage for the accrued leave days if he quits the work before using them, regardless of the leave duration, with respect to the period for which he did not obtain his leave. **He shall also be entitled to receive leave wage for parts of the year in proportion to the period he spent in work and it shall be calculated according to the basic wage.**" — [MOHRE PDF](https://mohre.gov.ae/assets/download/8cd7cf08/Federal%20Decree-Law%20No.%2033%20of%202021%20Regarding%20the%20Regulation%20of%20Employment%20Relationship%20and%20its%20amendments.pdf.aspx)
- **[P]** MOHRE's "Dear Worker – Know your rights" page: "You shall be entitled to a wage for the accrued leave days if you quit the work before using them, regardless of the leave duration, with respect to the period for which you did not obtain your leave. In this case, the employee will be entitled to payment for the unused days, calculated on the basis of his/her basic salary." ### Inferences
- The English version the project ingested appears to be the uaelegislation.gov.ae translation. MOHRE publishes a different English translation that includes the sentence. With the Arabic and two official English renderings (MOHRE PDF and MOHRE guidance) in agreement, the pro-rata, basic-wage rule is operative regardless of which language the agent answers in.
- For OQ-003 (decided: basic wage), the Arabic and MOHRE-English Art 29(9) and ER 19(2) all point to basic wage on termination. The u.ae carry-over sentence also supports basic wage during employment, but it is a paraphrase that conflicts with ER 19(1)'s "wage", so it is weak support.

### Gaps
- No court judgment was found that quotes or applies the second sentence of the new Art 29(9).
- The provenance and date of the ingested English text (presumably uaelegislation.gov.ae) were not re-checked against the live portal, which returns 403 to scripts.

## OQ-005: Boundaries in Art 29(1): exactly six months; part months under (b); "extended service" under (a)

### Takeaway
No official source settles any of the three sub-questions, and I found no court ruling.
1. **Exactly six months:** the statutory "more than six months" (تزيد على ستة أشهر) conflicts with the u.ae headline "if they have completed six months of service". Unresolved; the literal statutory reading is the safer default.
2. **Part months:** no source addresses rounding. Pro-rata is consistent with the Arabic Art 29(9) "بنسبة ما قضاه" on termination (low confidence).
3. **"Extended service":** u.ae renders (a) as 30 days once one year is completed, and (c) together with the Arabic 29(9) covers part years pro rata. So 30 days per completed year plus part years pro rata (medium confidence).

### Cited Findings
- **[P]** Statutory wording: (b) "يومان عن كل شهر إذا كانت مدة خدمته **تزيد على** ستة أشهر وتقل عن سنة" ("more than six months and less than one year"); (a) "ثلاثين يومًا عن كل سنة من سنوات خدمته الممتدة" — `labour-law:ar:29:1`. MOHRE's English: "a. Thirty days for each year of extended service; b. Two days for each month if his service term is more than six months and less than a year; c. A leave for parts of the last year he spent at work if his service is ended before using his annual leave balance." — [MOHRE PDF](https://mohre.gov.ae/assets/download/8cd7cf08/Federal%20Decree-Law%20No.%2033%20of%202021%20Regarding%20the%20Regulation%20of%20Employment%20Relationship%20and%20its%20amendments.pdf.aspx)
- **[P]** u.ae English headline: "Employees are entitled to an annual leave if they have **completed** six months of service." The body says: "Employees are entitled to a fully paid annual leave of 30 days, if they have completed one year of service and if the period of service **exceeds** six months but is less than one year, the employee is entitled to 2 days of leave for each month of service. If the employee's service ends before using their annual leave, they are entitled to leave for the fraction of the last year worked." — [u.ae EN (Wayback 25 Jul 2026)](https://web.archive.org/web/20260725160932/https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/employment-in-the-private-sector/types-of-leaves-and-entitlements-in-the-private-sector/annual-leave)
- **[P]** u.ae Arabic headline: "يحق للعامل الحصول على إجازة سنوية إذا **أكمل** مدّة 6 أشهر خدمة لدى صاحب العمل". Body: "30 يوما عن كل سنة من سنوات خدمته / يومين عن كل شهر إذا كانت مدة خدمته **تزيد على** ستة أشهر، وتقل عن سنة". The Arabic body drops "الممتدة". — [u.ae AR (Wayback 9 Nov 2025)](https://web.archive.org/web/20251109013543/https://u.ae/ar/information-and-services/jobs/employment-in-the-private-sector/types-of-leaves-and-entitlements-in-the-private-sector/annual-leave)
- **[P]** MOHRE's guidance repeats the statute verbatim and does not resolve the boundary: "Thirty days for each year of your extended service. Two days per month in respect of any employee with more than six months and less than one year of service." — [MOHRE Dear Worker (Wayback)](https://web.archive.org/web/20260118075825/https://mohre.gov.ae/en/guidance-and-awareness-portal-new/employee-companies/dear-worker-know-your-rights). MOHRE's Arabic guidance as reported in the press says the same ("ثلاثين يوماً عن كل سنة من سنوات الخدمة الممتدة، ويومين عن كل شهر إذا كانت مدة الخدمة تزيد على ستة أشهر وتقل عن سنة") — [Emarat Alyoum, 1 Aug 2025](https://www.emaratalyoum.com/local-section/other/2025-08-01-1.1963025)
- **[S]** Gulf News, attributing it to MOHRE guidance: "once a worker has completed six months of service, he or she can avail of two days of annual leave per month of service", until one year. Its photo caption says workers need "at least six months of continuous service to be eligible to take annual leave." — [Gulf News, Huda Tabrez, updated 11 Aug 2023](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/working-in-the-uae-if-you-havent-completed-a-year-of-service-can-you-still-take-annual-leave-1.1637675620021)
- **[S]** Secondary guides read the band as opening only after six months; for example, the "2-days-a-month band applies once the service term is more than six months" — [payslip.ae](https://payslip.ae/annual-leave); [Sovereign Group](https://www.sovereigngroup.com/news/a-comprehensive-guide-to-leave-entitlements-in-the-uae/)
- **[S]** Sovereign Group says accrual is two days a month "(or 1.83 days per month if using the 'working day' calculation)", which suggests some practitioners convert the leave to working days — [Sovereign Group](https://www.sovereigngroup.com/news/a-comprehensive-guide-to-leave-entitlements-in-the-uae/). By contrast, Gulf HR says the 30-day entitlement is in calendar days — [gulfhr.ae](https://gulfhr.ae/insights/uae-annual-leave-rules-carry-forward-encashment-forfeiture/) (both secondary and in conflict)
- **[S, historical]** The repealed Federal Law No. 8 of 1980 Art 75 had the same threshold ("يومين عن كل شهر، إذا كانت مدة خدمة العامل تزيد على ستة أشهر وتقل عن السنة، و30 يوماً عن كل سنة إذا كانت مدة خدمة العامل تزيد على سنة"). A Ministry of Labour legal source added that on termination the worker "يستحق إجازة سنوية عن كسور سنة الخدمة الأخيرة". — [Emarat Alyoum "المرشد العمالي", 9 May 2014](https://www.emaratalyoum.com/local-section/other/2014-05-09-1.674214)

### Inferences
- **Sub-question 1, exactly six months.** The statute says "more than" in both languages. The u.ae and Gulf News "completed six months" wording reads more like eligibility to start *taking* leave than a deliberate reading of the boundary. Recommended: keep the literal reading (exactly six months does not trigger (b)), flag it, and note that the u.ae headline supports the other reading. Confidence: low–medium.
- **Effect of passing six months.** u.ae says "2 days of leave for each month of service", without excluding the first six months. That supports counting all months, including months 1–6, once the threshold is crossed (seven months gives 14 days). This matches the tool's current "service days × 2 ÷ 30". Inference, medium confidence.
- **Sub-question 2, part months.** No official or court source found. Pro-rata (days × 2 ÷ 30) is consistent with Arabic 29(9) "بنسبة ما قضاه منها في العمل" when service ends. During employment, a "completed months" reading is equally arguable because (b) says "عن كل شهر". Low confidence either way.
- **Sub-question 3, "extended service".** u.ae paraphrases (a) as "30 days, if they have completed one year", and (c) and the Arabic 29(9) separately cover "parts of the last year" pro rata. So the best reading is 30 days per completed year, with the part of the final year pro rata on termination. The statute does not fix the pro-rata rate for a part year after year one (30/365 per day, 2.5 per month, or the (b) rate of 2 per month). Because (b) is limited to total service under one year, the 30-day annual rate pro-rated is the more coherent reading. That is an inference; no source states it.
- The 1980 law had the same "more than six months" threshold and gave fractions of the last year on termination, and MOHRE's legal source glossed it the same way in 2014. The 2021 drafters kept that structure, which suggests continuity rather than a new rule.

### Gaps
- No MOHRE FAQ, calculator output or court judgment was found on exactly 180 days, on rounding part months, or on the rate for a part year after the first year. The mohre.gov.ae calculators could not be reached (connection refused).
- Whether full-time leave days are calendar or working days has no official source; the secondary sources conflict.

## OQ-010: How ER Art 18 converts part-time hours into annual leave

### Takeaway
Official guidance only restates ER Art 18, and I found no worked example from MOHRE and no court ruling. All three sub-questions are unresolved: the five-day minimum for part years, the level at which a part day is rounded, and working days versus calendar days. The tool's current approach (full-time entitlement × hours percentage, floor of 5/30, total rounded up to a whole day) is a defensible reading, but it is an inference.

### Cited Findings
- **[P]** ER Art 18's formula: total working hours converted to working days, ÷ working days per year, × the statutory leave, "بحد أدنى خمسة أيام عمل في السنة", "يعد الجزء من اليوم يومًا كاملاً". Actual hours count at most 8 a day; the percentage is yearly part-time hours ÷ yearly full-time hours × 100. — `executive-regulations:ar:18:1`, `executive-regulations:en:18:1`
- **[P]** MOHRE "Dear Worker" restates it without an example: "its duration is determined based on the total working hours converted to working days, divided by the number of working days per year, multiplied by the legally prescribed leaves, at a minimum of five working days per year for annual leave." — [MOHRE Dear Worker (Wayback)](https://web.archive.org/web/20260118075825/https://mohre.gov.ae/en/guidance-and-awareness-portal-new/employee-companies/dear-worker-know-your-rights)
- **[P]** MOHRE's guidance post as reported in May 2022 repeats the same text, including "يعد الجزء من اليوم يوماً كاملاً" and the four clauses, with no example. — [Emarat Alyoum, 16 May 2022](https://www.emaratalyoum.com/local-section/other/2022-05-16-1.1631145)
- **[P]** u.ae only says part-time employees get leave "according to the actual working hours they spent at work, as specified in the employment contract" and refers to the Implementing Regulations. — [u.ae EN (Wayback)](https://web.archive.org/web/20260725160932/https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/employment-in-the-private-sector/types-of-leaves-and-entitlements-in-the-private-sector/annual-leave)
- **[S]** Khaleej Times paraphrases the steps ("Convert the working hours into full working days ... Divide it by the number of working days per year ... Multiply it by the number of entitled days off ... (a minimum of five working days a year)"). It gives no example and no rounding rule. — [Khaleej Times, 18 Feb 2022](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/jobs/new-uae-labour-law-how-to-calculate-gratuity-annual-leaves-for-part-time-jobs)
- **[S]** ProConsult's example: a half-time worker "would typically get about 15 calendar days" out of 30, "subject to the exact regulation". — [uaeahead.com](https://uaeahead.com/uae-labour-law-annual-leave-complete-guide-to-entitlement-calculation-and-rights/)
- **[S]** Gulf News quotes the ER text, including the five-day minimum and the part-day rule. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/new-uae-labour-law-decree-how-do-part-time-workers-get-annual-leave-1.1647351078774)

### Inferences
- **Conversion.** Contracted hours, capped at 8 a day (clause 2), ÷ 8 give working-day equivalents. Dividing by full-time working days a year and multiplying by the statutory leave (30 days, or the (b)/(c) amount) gives percentage × full-time entitlement, the same result as clause 4. ProConsult's half-time = 15 days example [S] is consistent with this.
- **Sub-question 1, five-day floor for part years.** The text says "per annum" (في السنة). Three readings:
  - (A) Pro-rate the floor (5 × service fraction). This is the tool's current approach and is coherent with the "per annum" wording.
  - (B) Apply the full 5 days to any part year. This is more generous but would give a one-month part-timer more than a full-timer's (b) accrual.
  - (C) No floor for part years.

  No source decides between them. A is the most coherent; low confidence.
- **Sub-question 2, rounding.** "وفي حساب هذه الإجازات المستحقة يعد الجزء من اليوم يومًا كاملاً" attaches the rounding to calculating "these due leaves", meaning the computed entitlement. Rounding the final total up is the natural reading. Rounding each year separately is possible for multi-year service but has no support. Low–medium confidence.
- **Sub-question 3, working vs calendar days.** ER 18 expresses the floor and the conversion in "أيام عمل" (working days) but multiplies by "الإجازات المقررة قانونًا", which Art 29(1) states as plain "يومًا" (days). The text therefore mixes units. A defensible reading: the ratio is unit-free (hours ÷ hours), so the result takes the unit of the full-time entitlement, and only the five-day floor is expressly in working days. Low confidence; no official source.

### Gaps
- No MOHRE worked example, calculator output, FAQ or judgment was found for part-time leave. The mohre.gov.ae calculator pages could not be reached.
- No source addresses part-time workers with under six months' service (where OQ-010 and OQ-011 overlap).

## OQ-011: Does a worker with six months' service or less earn annual leave or leave pay on termination?

### Takeaway
Unresolved, and the sources conflict. The government's own plain-language guidance (u.ae) says leave entitlement requires six months' service, and secondary guides agree. Three textual points support pro-rata leave pay on termination for any part year:
- Art 29(1)(c), "parts of the last year".
- The Arabic and MOHRE-English Art 29(9), pro-rata pay "for parts of the year" with no minimum.
- Art 29(3), which preserves compensation for the "remainder of his annual leave balance" for a worker who fails probation. Probation cannot exceed six months.

No court ruling was found. Recommendation: keep "no leave under six months" as the default, since it is the only reading an official guidance page states outright. Flag OQ-011 and present the pro-rata reading as credible. Confidence: low–medium.

### Inferences
- **Reading A: nothing for six months or less.** Support: Art 29(1)(b) sets the earliest accrual band at "more than six months"; u.ae's headline says entitlement needs six completed months; secondary guides agree; and the 1980 law had the same threshold. Under this reading, (c) and 29(9)'s "parts of the year" apply only to service that has already crossed the threshold, for example the part of a final year after one or more full years, or a worker with 6–12 months.
- **Reading B: pro-rata leave pay for any service on termination.** Support:
  - (c) and the Arabic/MOHRE-English 29(9) contain no minimum.
  - 29(9)'s "أيًّا كانت مدتها" ("regardless of their duration") is weak support, because "مدتها" refers to the leave days, not the length of service.
  - Strongest: Art 29(3) contemplates an "annual leave balance" during probation, and compensation for its remainder when the worker fails probation. Probation is at most six months under the Law (OQ-013 concerns how those six months are counted), so the Law contemplates a leave balance, and its encashment, for workers with six months' service or less.
  - Weakness: 29(3) can also be read as covering only leave the employer chose to advance, with "balance" meaning that advance. The words "the remainder of his annual leave balance" fit an accrued balance better.
- Nothing found shows how MOHRE or the courts actually decide this. Reading A matches the only official sentence directly on point (the u.ae headline). Reading B has the better textual argument from 29(3) and 29(9). Both should be presented when answering. If a single tool default is needed, keep A (as now) with the OQ-011 flag. If the project prefers the worker-protective reading, B means pro-rata leave pay, on basic wage, for service under six months.
