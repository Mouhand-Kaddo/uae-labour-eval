# Official texts firmly settle three of ten questions

Only three of the ten open questions (OQ-004 to OQ-013) have an answer the official sources state outright. **The Arabic Art 29(9) pro-rata leave-pay rule is operative** (OQ-004). **The Art 60 fine tops out at AED 1,000,000** (OQ-007). **Temporary work of one year or more earns gratuity** (OQ-006, first sub-question). In each case the prevailing Arabic text agrees with MOHRE's or u.ae's own English wording. Probation (OQ-013) comes next: Art 67's "month shall be deemed as thirty (30) days" makes six months 180 days on a plain reading, but no ministry or court source confirms it, so confidence is medium. The other six (part-time and flexible gratuity, the annual-leave boundaries, part-time leave, leave under six months, out-of-range notice periods) rest on reading the text with no ministry worked example and no court ruling. For those, the recommendations below are defensible defaults that keep their OQ flag, not settled law. On the agent-side facts, there are exactly three amending decree-laws (14/2022, 20/2023, 9/2024), the Executive Regulations have never been amended, and no amendment after 29 July 2024 was found. The Art 68(2) conversion deadline was extended once, to 31 December 2023. The Ministry of Justice publishes a consolidated Arabic compilation, current to December 2025, that scripts can fetch. The main limitation is access. MOHRE's live site, the legislation portal and every court database refused or blocked this research, so "no ruling found" means "none reachable", not "none exists".

How to read the evidence labels: "primary" means the Law or Executive Regulations (ER) themselves, or official government publications of them (Ministry of Justice compilation, MOHRE, u.ae). ## Three questions the official texts settle

### OQ-004: the Arabic pro-rata sentence is the law in both languages

**Recommended decision:** the agent states the pro-rata, basic-wage rule when answering in English, citing Art 29(9) and noting that the Arabic text prevails. English `cite` cases expect it. **Confidence: high.**

**Key primary text.** The Arabic Art 29(9) (ingested chunk `labour-law:ar:29:2`) ends: "كما يستحق أجرة الإجازة عن أجزاء السنة بنسبة ما قضاه منها في العمل، وتحسب وفق الأجر الأساسي" ([Law AR](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1541/download)). MOHRE's own English translation of the Law includes it: "He shall also be entitled to receive leave wage for parts of the year in proportion to the period he spent in work and it shall be calculated according to the basic wage" ([MOHRE consolidated EN PDF](https://web.archive.org/web/20260302100310id_/https://mohre.gov.ae/assets/download/8cd7cf08/Federal%20Decree-Law%20No.%2033%20of%202021%20Regarding%20the%20Regulation%20of%20Employment%20Relationship%20and%20its%20amendments.pdf.aspx)).

**Evidence.** The missing sentence is a gap in one translation, the portal's English text that the project ingested. **Three separate official English renderings carry it.** MOHRE's "Dear Worker" page tells workers they are "entitled to receive leave wage for parts of the year in proportion to the period you spent in work, calculated according to the basic wage" ([MOHRE Dear Worker](https://web.archive.org/web/20260118075825/https://mohre.gov.ae/en/guidance-and-awareness-portal-new/employee-companies/dear-worker-know-your-rights)). The u.ae English page says the employee "is also entitled to payment for leave accrued for part of the year, calculated on the basis of his/her basic salary" ([u.ae EN](https://web.archive.org/web/20260725160932/https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/employment-in-the-private-sector/types-of-leaves-and-entitlements-in-the-private-sector/annual-leave)), and the Arabic u.ae page repeats the statute ([u.ae AR](https://web.archive.org/web/20251109013543/https://u.ae/ar/information-and-services/jobs/employment-in-the-private-sector/types-of-leaves-and-entitlements-in-the-private-sector/annual-leave)). The sentence also supports the OQ-003 decision (basic wage) for leave cashed out on termination.

**Still unverified.** No court judgment applying the sentence was found. The sentence also does not settle whether "parts of the year" covers a first and only year of six months or less; that is OQ-011.

**Effect on tools and cases.** No calculation change: the annual-leave tool already pays part years pro rata on basic wage. For `cite` cases, the English expected answer includes the pro-rata rule. Consider ingesting MOHRE's English PDF as a second English source for Art 29(9). Note that this PDF dates from January 2024, before Decree-Law 9/2024, so its Arts 54 and 60 are out of date and must not be used for those articles.

### OQ-007: the Art 60 fine is AED 1,000,000

**Recommended decision:** the Art 60(1) maximum is AED 1,000,000. `cite` cases quote the Arabic or the numeral and never penalise "one million". **Confidence: high.**

**Key primary text.** Arabic Art 60(1): "يُعاقب بالغرامة التي لا تقل عن (100,000) مائة ألف درهم ولا تزيد على (1,000,000) مليون درهم" ([Law AR](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1541/download)). The English Art 60(2), a few lines later, says "not more than AED (1,000,000) one million" ([Law EN](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541/download)).

**Evidence.** Every indicator except one English word points the same way: the prevailing Arabic words and numerals, the English numeral "(1,000,000)" in the same sentence, the parallel clause 60(2), and DLA Piper's 2024 summary of the same offences as "fines ranging from AED100,000 to AED1 million" ([DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2024/UAE-government-introduces-increased-fines-and-criminal-penalties-for-violations-of-the-UAE-Labour-Law-)). The same English sentence also contains the typo "on hundred thousand", which shows how carelessly it was produced. The consolidated text already includes the 9/2024 version of Art 60, with its new fictitious-employment clauses (2)–(4).

**Still unverified.** The standalone text of Decree-Law 9/2024, the Art 60(1) range before 2024, and whether MOHRE's own post-2024 English translation repeats "two million". MOHRE's newer consolidated PDFs timed out ([MOHRE EN PDF](https://www.mohre.gov.ae/assets/download/e82f7872/Federal%20Decree-Law%20No.%2033%20of%202021%20Regarding%20the%20Regulation%20of%20Employment%20Relationship%20and%20its%20amendments_638990571068264034.pdf.aspx)). The timestamp in that URL decodes to 18 November 2025, so it is probably the post-2024 consolidation (an inference).

**Effect on tools and cases.** No calculation tool uses Art 60. Record the decision, and the penalty `cite` cases can drop `depends_on: [OQ-007]`.

### OQ-006: temporary work of a year or more earns gratuity

**Recommended decision:** (1) temporary work of one year or more earns gratuity; (2) compute it as the Art 51 gratuity × (contracted annual hours ÷ full-time annual hours), which equals plain Art 51 for full-time hours; (3) part-time and job-sharing workers with under a year get nothing, but because of Art 51(2)–(3), not ER 30(2). **Confidence: high for (1), low–medium for (2), medium–high for (3).**

**Key primary text.** The prevailing Arabic ER Art 30(2) contains one conditional exclusion with no "or": "لا تنطبق مكافأة نهاية الخدمة في حال العمل بنمط التوظيف المؤقت إذا كانت مدته أقل من سنة" ([ER AR](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1547/download)). Law Art 51(2)–(3) gives a full-time foreign worker gratuity only after "one or more years of continuous service" ([Law EN](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541/download)).

**Evidence.** The deciding fact is that **the government's own English guidance follows the Arabic, not the English ER.** MOHRE's English page says "The end-of-service gratuity does not apply to temporary workers with less than one year service" ([MOHRE Dear Worker](https://web.archive.org/web/20260118075825/https://mohre.gov.ae/en/guidance-and-awareness-portal-new/employee-companies/dear-worker-know-your-rights)). u.ae says gratuity "does not apply in cases of temporary employment, if the duration is less than one year" ([u.ae EN gratuity page](https://web.archive.org/web/20260501200625/https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/employment-in-the-private-sector/end-of-service-benefits-for-employees-in-the-private-sector)). Sub-question 1 is therefore settled.

Sub-question 2 has no source. The recommended method rests on two points: ER Art 30 is the only mechanism made under Law Art 52 for non-full-time patterns, and Art 7(1)(c) defines temporary work by duration or task, not hours. The method gives exactly Art 51 for a temporary worker on full-time hours, and the same pro-rating as part-time for one on reduced hours. The two candidate readings (Art 51 as for full-time, or ER 30(1) pro-rata) therefore differ only for temporary workers on reduced hours.

For sub-question 3, ER 30(1) multiplies "the value of the end of service pay under a full-time employment contract", which is zero before one year under Art 51. A part-time or job-sharing worker under one year gets nothing under either language version. MOHRE's 2022 statement, as reported in the press, gives a 12-month minimum for these patterns ([Emarat Al Youm](https://www.emaratalyoum.com/local-section/careers/2022-09-16-1.1668187)). Its wording "لم تتجاوز 12 شهراً" would deny gratuity at exactly 12 months, but the regulation's "أقل من سنة" and Art 51's "one or more years" both grant it, and the regulation should prevail over a press report.

**Still unverified.** Any official method for temporary gratuity of a year or more, any judgment on ER 30(2), and the original MOHRE tweet.

**Effect on tools and cases.** The gratuity tool should stop returning no gratuity for all temporary work. Temporary under one year: no gratuity, citing ER 30(2). Temporary of one year or more: Art 51 × hours ratio, still returning OQ-006 for the method. Part-time or job sharing under one year: no gratuity, citing Art 51(2)–(3) with ER 30(1). Several `depends_on: [OQ-006]` tags can be removed, because both language readings give the same answer: temporary work under a year, and part-time or job sharing under a year. Temporary of one year or more on full-time hours depends only on sub-question 1, so it can come off once the owner records the decision. Only temporary work of one year or more on reduced hours keeps OQ-006 (and picks up OQ-008).

## Gratuity for part-time and flexible work rests on reasoning alone

### OQ-008: pro-rate a full-time-equivalent wage, not the reduced one

**Recommended decision:** Option B. Apply the hours percentage to a gratuity computed on the full-time-equivalent basic wage. When the worker's own wage is proportionate to their hours, that equals Art 51 applied directly to the worker's own basic wage. **Confidence: low–medium.**

**Key primary text.** ER Art 30(1) multiplies the hours percentage by "the value of the end of service pay under a full-time employment contract" ([ER EN](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1547/download)), and the Arabic "قيمة مكافأة نهاية الخدمة لعقد العمل بالدوام الكامل" names no wage either ([ER AR](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1547/download)). ER Art 5(1)(b) says a job-sharer's wage already reflects the share, "بحيث يكون نسبة وتناسب", and job sharers are treated "وفق ضوابط العمل الجزئي".

**Evidence.** No official source names the wage. MOHRE guidance, u.ae and MOHRE's 2022 statement all repeat the formula without one ([MOHRE Dear Worker](https://web.archive.org/web/20260118075825/https://mohre.gov.ae/en/guidance-and-awareness-portal-new/employee-companies/dear-worker-know-your-rights); [u.ae EN](https://web.archive.org/web/20260501200625/https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/employment-in-the-private-sector/end-of-service-benefits-for-employees-in-the-private-sector)). The arithmetic decides the stakes. If a part-time wage is r × the full-time wage, Option A pays r² × the full-time gratuity and Option B pays r ×. **At half time, A pays a quarter of the full-time figure and B pays half.** B matches the press illustration of "50% of the full-time gratuity entitlement" at 50% hours ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/new-uae-labour-law-are-part-time-workers-entitled-to-end-of-service-benefits-1.1652369188838)). It also fits the phrase "under a full-time employment contract" and Art 52's instruction to serve "the interests of both parties".

The strongest argument for A should be weighed honestly. Art 51(5) names only "the last basic wage" the worker was entitled to, and nothing authorises a notional wage. Under B, ER 30(1) changes nothing whenever the wage is already proportionate, and a court could resist reading a regulation as having no effect. That is why confidence stays low–medium.

**Still unverified.** Any MOHRE calculator handling part-time hours (MOHRE's calculators were unreachable) and any judgment.

**Effect on tools and cases.** Today the tool applies the percentage to a gratuity on whatever wage it is given, which is Option A when given the worker's own wage. Under B it computes Art 51 on the worker's own basic wage when that wage is proportionate, or takes a full-time-equivalent wage and applies the percentage. Art 51 is linear in the wage, including the 24-month cap, so the two give the same figure. Keep returning OQ-008 with every part-time and job-sharing result, since no authority confirms the choice.

### OQ-009: flexible workers fall under the "other work patterns" formula

**Recommended decision:** compute a flexible worker's gratuity under ER Art 30(1), using contracted annual hours, with no gratuity under one year of continuous service (Art 51(2)–(3)). **Confidence: low–medium.**

**Key primary text.** The ER Art 30 heading is "مكافأة نهاية الخدمة للعاملين بأنماط العمل الأخرى" (gratuity for workers in other work patterns), and its opening clause covers part-time and job sharing "وبغير نظام العمل بدوام كامل" ([ER AR](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1547/download)). Law Art 52 delegates gratuity "for foreign Workers in work types other than full-time" to the ER, and Art 51 is headed "for Full-Time Workers" ([Law EN](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541/download)).

**Evidence.** The best official indication is u.ae, which applies the Art 30(1) formula to "part time contracts **or other types of work arrangements**" ([u.ae EN](https://web.archive.org/web/20260501200625/https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/employment-in-the-private-sector/end-of-service-benefits-for-employees-in-the-private-sector)). MOHRE's English page is narrower, mentioning only part-time and job sharing ([MOHRE Dear Worker](https://web.archive.org/web/20260118075825/https://mohre.gov.ae/en/guidance-and-awareness-portal-new/employee-companies/dear-worker-know-your-rights)). Reading "no gratuity at all" has no textual support. Art 52 tells the ER to *regulate* gratuity for other patterns, and the only exclusion the ER contains is temporary work under a year. Full Art 51 is barred by its own "full-time pattern" heading, and it gives the same figure as the Art 30(1) formula whenever the flexible worker in fact works full-time hours.

**Still unverified.** What "annual working hours stipulated in the employment contract" means for a contract whose hours vary by definition (Art 7(1)(d)). Using actual hours worked would be an extrapolation with no source.

**Effect on tools and cases.** Replace the `flexible_work_undecided` outcome with the Art 30(1) computation whenever the contract states annual hours. Keep "undecided" only when it does not. Keep returning OQ-009.

## Annual-leave boundaries remain genuinely open

The three annual-leave questions share one problem. **The statute, MOHRE's guidance and u.ae each phrase the six-month threshold differently, and no worked example or ruling exists.** The recommendations keep the tool close to the literal text and keep every flag.

### OQ-005: the tool's current readings are the defensible ones

**Recommended decision:** confirm the tool's current behaviour as the decision on all three sub-questions. Nothing under (b) at exactly 180 days. Part months under (b) pro rata (service days × 2 ÷ 30), counting all months from day one once the threshold is crossed. 30 days per completed year, plus part years pro rata at 30/365. **Confidence: low–medium for sub-questions 1 and 2; medium for sub-question 3.**

**Key primary text.** Art 29(1)(b): "يومان عن كل شهر إذا كانت مدة خدمته **تزيد على** ستة أشهر وتقل عن سنة". MOHRE's English renders this as "more than six months and less than a year" ([MOHRE consolidated EN PDF](https://web.archive.org/web/20260302100310id_/https://mohre.gov.ae/assets/download/8cd7cf08/Federal%20Decree-Law%20No.%2033%20of%202021%20Regarding%20the%20Regulation%20of%20Employment%20Relationship%20and%20its%20amendments.pdf.aspx)). Art 29(1)(a): "ثلاثين يومًا عن كل سنة من سنوات خدمته الممتدة" ([Law AR](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1541/download)).

**Evidence.** On the exact six-month boundary, the statute says "more than" in both languages, but u.ae's headline says employees are entitled "if they have **completed** six months of service". Its body then reverts to "exceeds six months" ([u.ae EN](https://web.archive.org/web/20260725160932/https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/employment-in-the-private-sector/types-of-leaves-and-entitlements-in-the-private-sector/annual-leave)). The headline reads more like a summary of when leave can first be *taken* than a deliberate ruling on day 180, so the literal reading stays. The repealed 1980 law used the same "more than six months" threshold, which suggests continuity rather than a new rule ([Emarat Al Youm, 2014](https://www.emaratalyoum.com/local-section/other/2014-05-09-1.674214)).

On part months, no source speaks. Pro-rata fits Art 29(9)'s "بنسبة ما قضاه" on termination. During employment, a "completed months" reading is equally arguable.

On "extended service", u.ae paraphrases (a) as "30 days, if they have completed one year of service". (c) and the Arabic 29(9) separately cover "parts of the last year". That supports 30 days per completed year with the final part year pro rata. The rate for that part year is an inference: 30/365 is more coherent than (b)'s 2 days a month, because (b) is limited to total service under a year. Secondary sources conflict on whether the 30 days are calendar or working days ([Sovereign Group](https://www.sovereigngroup.com/news/a-comprehensive-guide-to-leave-entitlements-in-the-uae/) vs [Gulf HR](https://gulfhr.ae/insights/uae-annual-leave-rules-carry-forward-encashment-forfeiture/)), and no official source settles it.

**Still unverified.** Any MOHRE calculator output, FAQ or judgment on day 180, rounding, or the part-year rate.

**Effect on tools and cases.** No code change. Cases at exactly 180 days and cases that rely on pro-rated part months keep `depends_on: [OQ-005]` until the owner records these readings. Cases on whole years only can drop it. If the owner later adopts reading B for OQ-011, sub-question 1 becomes moot, because day 180 would earn pro-rata leave anyway.

### OQ-010: keep the tool's part-time leave conversion

**Recommended decision:** confirm the current method: full-time entitlement × hours percentage, a five-day floor pro-rated for part years, the final total rounded up to a whole day, and the result in the same units as the full-time entitlement. **Confidence: low.**

**Key primary text.** ER Art 18: "...مقسومًا على عدد أيام العمل في السنة، مضروبًا في الإجازات المقررة قانونًا، وذلك بحد أدنى خمسة أيام عمل في السنة للإجازات السنوية، وفي حساب هذه الإجازات المستحقة يعد الجزء من اليوم يومًا كاملاً" ([ER AR](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1547/download)).

**Evidence.** MOHRE restates the ER text without an example ([MOHRE Dear Worker](https://web.archive.org/web/20260118075825/https://mohre.gov.ae/en/guidance-and-awareness-portal-new/employee-companies/dear-worker-know-your-rights); [Emarat Al Youm, 2022](https://www.emaratalyoum.com/local-section/other/2022-05-16-1.1631145)), and u.ae only refers back to the regulations. The conversion itself (hours capped at 8 a day, then ÷ working days × statutory leave) reduces to percentage × full-time entitlement, consistent with one firm's half-time = 15 days example ([uaeahead.com](https://uaeahead.com/uae-labour-law-annual-leave-complete-guide-to-entitlement-calculation-and-rights/)).

On the five-day floor for part years, "في السنة" (per year) makes pro-rating the most coherent reading. Applying the full five days to a one-month part-timer would give them more than a full-timer accrues under (b). On rounding, the round-up attaches to "هذه الإجازات المستحقة" (these due leaves), the computed entitlement, so rounding the total is the natural reading. On units, the ratio is hours ÷ hours and so has no unit of its own, which means the result takes the unit of the full-time entitlement. Only the floor is expressly in working days.

**Still unverified.** Everything beyond the formula. No worked example, calculator or judgment was found, and no source covers part-timers under six months, where OQ-010 overlaps OQ-011.

**Effect on tools and cases.** No code change; keep returning OQ-010 with every part-time result. Cases on a whole year at a ratio whose result clears the five-day floor and is already a whole day do not depend on sub-questions 1 and 2. They still depend on sub-question 3 if the expected answer names the unit (working or calendar days).

### OQ-011: leave under six months is the hardest call

**Recommended decision:** provisionally keep "no leave for six months or less" as the tool default, and record that the opposite reading is textually stronger. **Confidence: low.**

**Key primary text.** Art 29(3) keeps, for a worker who fails probation, "حقه في التعويض عما تبقى من رصيد إجازته السنوية" ("his right to compensation for the remainder of his annual leave balance"). Art 29(9) in Arabic adds pro-rata pay "عن أجزاء السنة" with no minimum ([Law AR](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1541/download)).

**Evidence.** The government's plain-language headline is the only official sentence directly on point, and it supports reading A: "Employees are entitled to an annual leave if they have completed six months of service" ([u.ae EN](https://web.archive.org/web/20260725160932/https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/employment-in-the-private-sector/types-of-leaves-and-entitlements-in-the-private-sector/annual-leave)). Secondary guides agree that "there is no statutory entitlement to paid annual leave if the service term is less than six months" ([Sovereign Group](https://www.sovereigngroup.com/news/a-comprehensive-guide-to-leave-entitlements-in-the-uae/)).

The same u.ae page, though, also says without any minimum that a leaver is "entitled to leave for the fraction of the last year worked". Art 29(3) is the strongest point for reading B. Probation cannot exceed six months (Art 9(1)), yet the Law speaks of a leave "balance" during probation and of compensation for its remainder when the worker fails. That only makes sense if leave accrues before six months, unless "balance" means only leave the employer chose to advance. MOHRE's guidance repeats the probation-compensation sentence ([MOHRE Dear Worker](https://web.archive.org/web/20260118075825/https://mohre.gov.ae/en/guidance-and-awareness-portal-new/employee-companies/dear-worker-know-your-rights)). Reading A follows the only explicit official statement. Reading B follows the structure of Art 29.

**Still unverified.** How MOHRE dispute decisions or the courts actually rule. Two MOHRE complaint-report links surfaced by a search did not load, and the court databases were not reached.

**Effect on tools and cases.** Keep the tool as it is and keep OQ-011 on every result under 180 days. Such cases cannot be marked reviewed until the owner decides. If the owner prefers reading B, the tool pays pro-rata leave on basic wage (days × 30 ÷ 365) for any service on termination, and OQ-005's sub-question 1 disappears with it.

## Notice and probation follow the text, with no practice to confirm it

### OQ-012: the worker-favourable split for out-of-range notice

**Recommended decision:** a contract notice term outside 30–90 days that hurts the worker in the termination at hand is void and replaced by the nearest limit. One that helps the worker stands. Pay in lieu is the last wage for whichever period applies. **Confidence: medium for the first part, low for the second.**

**Key primary text.** Art 43(1) bounds the agreed period: "على ألا تقل المدة عن (30) ثلاثين يومًا، ولا تزيد على (90) تسعين يومًا". Art 43(2) allows an asymmetric period: "ويشترط أن تكون مدة الإنذار واحدة للطرفين ما لم تكن لصالح العامل". Art 65(3) voids contrary terms "ما لم يكن أكثر فائدة للعامل" ([Law AR](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1541/download)). Art 43(3)–(4) sets pay in lieu at "the Worker's wage for the entire warning period or the remaining part of it", calculated "according to the last wage received by the Worker" ([Law EN](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541/download)).

**Evidence.** No official guidance, ER provision or judgment addresses an out-of-range clause. u.ae restates the 30–90 band and the worker-favourable exception without a rule for violations ([u.ae](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/Sector-of-employment/employment-in-the-private-sector/terminating-employment-contracts)). Commentary says a clause over 90 days is "generally considered invalid" but not what replaces it ([Al Ramsy](https://alramsyadvocates.com/notice-period-uae/)), or relies on Art 65 that "the conditions that are more beneficial to the employee shall apply" ([Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/the-notice-period-under-uae-federal-decree-by-law-no-33-of-2021-regulating-labour-relations)). The split reading is the only one that gives effect to the "in the interest of the Worker" words in both Art 43(2) and Art 65(3). The worker-favourable half has no authority on either side, which is why it is low. In practice the four cases work out as follows:

| Contract says | Employer terminates | Worker resigns |
|---|---|---|
| 15 days | 30 days apply; pay in lieu = 30 days' wage (medium) | 15 days stand; liability ≤ 15 days' wage (low) |
| 120 days | 120 days stand; pay in lieu = 120 days' wage (low) | 90-day cap; liability ≤ 90 days' wage (medium) |

**Still unverified.** Whether MOHRE's e-contract system even accepts a period outside 30–90 days (if it does not, the question arises mainly from offer letters), and any case law.

**Effect on tools and cases.** The notice tool can stop withholding pay in lieu. It computes the applicable period by who terminates, uses the last wage per Art 43(4), and keeps returning OQ-012. Cases where the term hurts the worker get the same answer under the simple "snap to the nearest limit" reading and under the split. The owner's decision only changes the two low-confidence cells, so those are the cases that must keep `depends_on: [OQ-012]` if the owner wants to defer.

### OQ-013: six months of probation is 180 days

**Recommended decision:** Option A. Six months is 180 days. **Confidence: medium.**

**Key primary text.** Art 67 (AR): "يكون حساب المدد والمواعيد المنصوص عليها في هذا المرسوم بقانون بالتقويم الميلادي، وتعد السنة الميلادية في تطبيق أحكام هذا المرسوم بقانون (365) ثلاثمائة وخمس وستون يومًا والشهر (30) ثلاثون يومًا." Art 9(1) (AR): "مدة لا تزيد على (6) ستة أشهر من تاريخ مباشرة العمل" ([Law AR](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1541/download)).

**Evidence.** The 30-day deeming rule applies "in applying the provisions of this Decree-Law" to the periods "stipulated" in it, which includes Art 9's six months. The "Gregorian calendar" clause reads most naturally as excluding the Hijri calendar rather than requiring calendar-month counting. Commentary only repeats "up to six months" and never converts it to days ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/legal/uae-jobs-what-you-need-to-know-about-6-month-probation-period-after-new-employment-law-went-into-fo?amp=1); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=dcaef5ba-a5bd-45a4-8189-ef3b73abca9a)). The gap is small: six calendar months from day one run 181–184 days. 180 days is also the conservative answer to "is this probation lawful?", because it never lets an employer exceed either reading. It keeps the probation tool consistent with the 30-day month used elsewhere in the project.

**Still unverified.** Whether MOHRE's work-permit system records probation in days or months, and any judgment on the point.

**Effect on tools and cases.** Keep Option A. The probation tool should return OQ-013 only when the period ends between day 181 and six calendar months from the start date, not with every result. `depends_on: [OQ-013]` can be narrowed in the same way: cases wholly within 180 days, or beyond 184 days, do not depend on it. The doc's own example (1 January to 30 June, 181 days) sits inside the window and keeps the tag.

## Facts to verify: three amendments, one extension, no later changes

**Amendment dates.** The Ministry of Justice's 2025 electronic compilation of labour legislation prints the consolidated Law with an amendment note against each changed provision. It attributes changes to exactly three instruments ([MOJ 2025 compilation](https://www.moj.gov.ae/assets/966b93b0/تشريعات-تنظيم-علاقات-العمل-2025-النسخة-الالكترونية-639015001687388768.aspx)).

| Instrument | Issued | Gazette | In force | Changed | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Decree-Law 14/2022 | 15 Sep 2022 | No. 736, 28 Sep 2022 | 29 Sep 2022 | Art 8(3): removed the 3-year cap on fixed-term contracts | Article changed: primary (MOJ). Dates: secondary (Eastlaws index title, Lexis) |
| Decree-Law 20/2023 | **13 Sep 2023** | not found | **1 Jan 2024** | Art 54 (disputes; 1-year limitation) | Primary: the instrument itself (MOHRE AR/EN) |
| Decree-Law 9/2024 | 29 Jul 2024 | No. 780 (supplement), 31 Jul 2024 | 30 days after publication: **29, 30 or 31 Aug 2024** depending on the source | Arts 54 and 60, plus a transitional Art 2 sending pending appeals to first-instance courts | Articles changed: primary (MOJ). Dates: secondary |

Three corrections follow for `open-questions.md`. First, 20/2023 was signed on 13 September 2023 ("27 Safar 1445"), per its own text ([MOHRE AR](https://web.archive.org/web/20250909074946id_/https://mohre.gov.ae/assets/download/95f9a7d4/%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%B3%D9%88%D9%85%20%D8%A8%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86%20%D8%A7%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%AF%D9%8A%20%D8%B1%D9%82%D9%85%2020%20%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9%202023%20%D8%A8%D8%AA%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%84%20%D8%A8%D8%B9%D8%B6%20%D8%A3%D8%AD%D9%83%D8%A7%D9%85%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%B3%D9%88%D9%85%20%D8%A8%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86%20%D8%A7%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%AF%D9%8A%20%D8%B1%D9%82%D9%85%2033%20%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9%202021%20%D8%A8%D8%B4%D8%A3%D9%86%20%D8%AA%D9%86%D8%B8%D9%8A%D9%85%20%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%AA%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%85%D9%84.pdf.aspx)), not 15 September as Lexis gives it ([Lexis](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/law/UnitedArabEmirates/DecreeLaw_20_2023/en)). Its Art 2 sets "ويُعمل به اعتباراً من تاريخ 2024/01/01". Second, 14/2022's effective date is 29 September 2022, not its 15 September issue date ([Eastlaws listing](https://www.eastlaws.com/legislation-full-text/ar/uae/federal-decree/28-09-2022/no-14?type=1&id=2064121&sub=10488510), index title only). Third, no source found gives 28 August 2024 for 9/2024.

The reported dates are 29 August (MOHRE as reported, "إعمالاً لنص المادة الرابعة"; [Emarat Al Youm](https://www.emaratalyoum.com/local-section/other/2024-08-29-1.1878298)), 30 August ([Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2024/09/significant-amendments-to-the-uae-labour-law)) and 31 August ([Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cm0mihiiy008uvc5khrvmjvdz/key-amendments-to-the-uae-labour-law-federal-decree-law-no-9-of-2024); [DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2024/UAE-government-introduces-increased-fines-and-criminal-penalties-for-violations-of-the-UAE-Labour-Law-)). Counting 30 days from the day after the 31 July publication gives 30 August. Without the wording of 9/2024's Art 4, the corpus should say "30 days after publication in the Official Gazette on 31 July 2024" rather than name a day.

One consequence matters for `cite` cases. Under 20/2023, from 1 January 2024 until 9/2024 took effect, the limitation period was one year "from the date the right claimed became due". The current Art 54(9) bars claims "بعد مرور سنتين من تاريخ انتهاء علاقة العمل", two years from the end of employment ([MOJ 2025 compilation](https://www.moj.gov.ae/assets/966b93b0/تشريعات-تنظيم-علاقات-العمل-2025-النسخة-الالكترونية-639015001687388768.aspx)). Commentary still circulating the one-year rule is out of date.

**No later amendments.** The portal's Labour Law page showed "Legislation Amendments 3" and a last update of 29 July 2024 in every capture through 4 October 2026 ([portal, Wayback 2026-10-04](https://web.archive.org/web/20261004194241id_/https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541); [amendments tab, 2025-07-08](https://web.archive.org/web/20250708052812id_/https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1541/modifications)). The previously unchecked Executive Regulations page shows a last update of 3 February 2022 and **no amendments field at all** ([ER portal page, Wayback 2026-10-04](https://web.archive.org/web/20261004194241id_/https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1547)). The MOJ compilation of December 2025 carries no amendment notes on the ER. A blog claiming "2025 amendments" describes the 20/2023 rules and is unreliable. The window from 4 to 18 October 2026 is unchecked.

**Art 68 deadline.** Art 68 itself is unamended. MOHRE Ministerial Resolution No. 27 of 2023 extended the conversion deadline once, from early February 2023 to **31 December 2023**. The evidence is MOHRE's own news headline, known only from search snippets ([MOHRE news](https://www.mohre.gov.ae/en/media-centre/news/30/1/2023/until-end-of-2023-ministry-extends-deadline-to-rectify-unlimited-term-employment-contracts-in-private-sector-to-fixed-term-contracts.aspx)), and law-firm reports ([Squire Patton Boggs](https://www.squirepattonboggs.com/insights/publications/uae-extends-grace-period-for-employees-to-transition-to-fixed-term-contracts-to-31-december-2023/); [PwC](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/media-centre/articles/limited-term-contracts-deadline-extension.html)). No second extension was found. No official source says what happens to contracts that were never converted. HR blogs claiming they are "now treated as fixed-term" cite nothing. The agent's existing "not found" answer on Art 68(3) gratuity remains correct.

**Fetchable official copies.** The portal has no separate pages for the amending decree-laws; it stores them as "modifications" behind download URLs that return 403 to scripts. Three sets of official copies can be fetched:

- the **MOJ 2025 compilation**, live with HTTP 200, in Arabic, consolidated through 9/2024 and including the ER; this is the best single source for the current Arts 8(3), 54 and 60;
- **MOHRE's PDFs of 20/2023** in English and Arabic, via Wayback ([EN](https://web.archive.org/web/20250903095312id_/https://www.mohre.gov.ae/assets/download/20670ee7/Federal%20Decree%20Law%20No.%2020%20of%202023%20Amending%20Certain%20Provisions%20of%20Federal%20Decree%20Law%20No.%2033%20of%202021%20Regarding%20the%20Regulation%20of%20Employment%20Relationships.pdf.aspx)); the English copy carries a third-party translation disclaimer;
- **MOHRE's consolidated PDFs** from January 2024, via Wayback, which predate 9/2024.

No standalone official copy of 14/2022 or 9/2024 was found in either language, and no official English text of 9/2024 exists among the sources found.

## Conclusion

The pattern across the ten questions is that **the project's ingested English text is the weakest link, not the law.** Where the English disagrees with the Arabic (OQ-004, OQ-006, OQ-007), MOHRE's and u.ae's own English guidance sides with the Arabic every time. That makes MOHRE's English translation and the MOJ Arabic compilation worth adding to the corpus as cross-checks, with care over the date of each copy. Where the texts are silent rather than divergent (OQ-008 to OQ-012), no official source fills the gap. Recording an owner decision there is a project convention, not a finding about the law. Those cases should stay unreviewable or carry their flag in answers.

The cheapest gains come from narrowing `depends_on` rather than deciding hard questions. Probation needs only the 181–184-day window. Temporary and part-time work under a year needs no OQ-006 flag. Notice terms that hurt the worker resolve the same way under every reading. Together these take a meaningful share of cases out of the "cannot be reviewed" pool without the owner taking a contested legal position.

## Main limitation: sources that could not be reached

The researchers could not read any court decision, and they read MOHRE's own pages only through archived copies. The unreachable sources were:

- **mohre.gov.ae**, live: connections refused or timed out. This blocked its FAQs, its official end-of-service and leave calculators, the January 2023 news item on the Art 68 extension, the newer consolidated PDFs (uploaded around November 2025), and the MOHRE complaint back office.
- **uaelegislation.gov.ae**: 403 to scripts and a Cloudflare challenge to browsers. This blocked the modification downloads that would hold the texts of 14/2022 and 9/2024, including the commencement wording of 9/2024's Art 4.
- **Eastlaws**: 403, so the Gazette numbers are known only from search-index titles.
- **Court portals and legal databases**: the Federal Supreme Court, the Dubai and Abu Dhabi Courts of Cassation, and Al Meezan were not reached. A summary of a Court of Cassation leave case (AtlasHXM) failed to load.
- **Smaller gaps**: the original 2022 MOHRE tweet, Ministerial Resolution 27/2023 itself, the live u.ae annual-leave pages (now 404) and a government.ae mirror (TLS error).

Any of these could overturn a low-confidence recommendation. The highest-value next step is MOHRE's calculators and a cassation-database search on OQ-011 and OQ-012.
